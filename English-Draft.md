# Petition for the Enactment of a Comprehensive Data Protection Act  Governing Both Public and Private Sector Data Processing
Written By: Fishie, Mohamed Hoodh Ibrahim,
Legal Advice: Uz. Ahmed Thaail, Uza. Aishath Nuha Hameed

We, the citizens of the Republic of Maldives, respectfully submit this petition to address the critical lack of a modern, comprehensive framework to protect the fundamental rights, privacy, and digital security of the Maldivian people.

## 1. The Growing Risk to Citizens’ Rights and Safety
In the modern digital environment, personal data including identity information, contact details, financial records, location data, biometric identifiers, and communication metadata is continuously collected, processed, stored, and shared by both private entities and public authorities. 

The Maldivian state has increasingly adopted digital governance systems, including national authentication mechanisms, centralised databases, and ministry-operated online platforms. These systems now hold large volumes of highly sensitive personal data relating to citizens and residents.    

However, this expansion of digital systems has not been matched by a unified, modern, and enforceable data protection law. As a result, personal data is routinely processed without consistent legal standards, clear accountability, or effective oversight.
This legal gap exposes citizens to serious risks, including identity theft, financial harm, unlawful surveillance, physical safety concerns arising from location data exposure, and long-term erosion of trust in digital government services.

## 2. Absence of Accountability and Breach Transparency
At present, there is no comprehensive legal obligation requiring organisations or public authorities to notify individuals when their personal data has been compromised.   

Data breaches involving sensitive information such as banking details, identity records, and location data may occur without affected individuals being informed in a timely manner, or at all. Citizens often learn of such incidents through informal channels, social media, or rumours, rather than through transparent and accountable processes.  

The absence of mandatory breach notification, investigation standards, and enforceable consequences undermines public confidence and leaves individuals unable to take protective action when their data is exposed.    

## 3. Systemic Misuse of Personal Data in Everyday Services

In addition to high-risk data exposures, citizens routinely experience misuse of personal data in everyday interactions with both public and private sector services.

Examples of such practices include, but are not limited to:

- Automatic enrolment into third-party services without clear and informed consent

- Continued promotional messaging and marketing communications despite opt-out requests or Do-Not-Disturb preferences

- Personal data being shared or repurposed beyond the original service context without explicit and informed consent

While these practices may appear less severe than large-scale data breaches, they reflect a broader structural issue: personal data is frequently treated as a commercial or administrative asset rather than as information entrusted by individuals under clearly defined legal safeguards.

These recurring issues highlight the absence of a consent-based and rights-driven data governance framework applicable across all sectors.

## 4. Emerging Risks from Artificial Intelligence and Automated Data Processing

Rapid advancements in artificial intelligence, automated decision-making systems, and data analytics have introduced new and complex risks to individual privacy and civil liberties.

Digital transformation initiatives, including expanding e-government services, national digital identity systems, and rapidly growing fintech and data-driven platforms, increasingly rely on large-scale data processing and algorithmic analysis.

While these technologies provide significant benefits to governance efficiency, service delivery, and economic development, their use also raises legitimate concerns that require clear legal safeguards. These include:

- The use of personal data for AI training, profiling, or automated decision-making without meaningful consent
- The processing of highly sensitive categories of data, including biometric, facial recognition, voice recognition, behavioural, and communications data
- The potential for disproportionate or opaque surveillance and profiling practices that may infringe constitutional rights and civil liberties

A modern data protection framework can support responsible innovation while ensuring that technological advancement occurs within clear legal, ethical, and rights-based boundaries.

## 5. Inadequacy of the Current Legal Framework
The existing legal landscape in the Maldives is fragmented and insufficient to address the realities of modern data processing. There is no single, comprehensive statute that:

- Applies uniformly to both public and private sector data processing
- Defines enforceable rights for individuals over their personal data
- Establishes clear lawful bases for data collection, use, and retention
- Regulates the processing of sensitive and biometric data
- Mandates breach notification, mitigation, and remediation standards
- Provides independent oversight with effective investigative and enforcement powers

This gap is particularly concerning in relation to state-operated digital systems, as public authorities process data at a scale and sensitivity that demands the highest level of legal protection and accountability.

## 6. Urgency of Legislative Action
The urgency of establishing a comprehensive data protection framework has significantly increased due to:

- The rapid national expansion of digital government services and centralised citizen platforms
- Accelerating growth in the fintech and digital services sector
- Increasing reliance on artificial intelligence, automated systems, and large-scale data analytics
- The growing volume and sensitivity of personal data being collected across both public and private sectors
- Multiple data leaks from private and government services. 
- Companies refusal to disclose breaches in a responsible manner. 

Failure to establish clear legal protections in parallel with this growth increases systemic risk to citizens, weakens trust in digital transformation initiatives, and exposes national infrastructure to long-term governance and security challenges.

## 7. Request to the People’s Majlis
In light of the above, we respectfully request the Honourable People’s Majlis to:
Take the necessary legislative steps to consider, deliberate upon, and enact a dedicated and comprehensive Data Protection Act, building upon draft data protection legislation that has existed within the legislative process since 2008, and ensure that the resulting law applies to all entities processing personal data within the Republic of Maldives, including public authorities and private organisations.


Ensure that such legislation establishes enforceable rights for individuals, clear obligations for data controllers and processors, mandatory breach notification requirements, and independent oversight with effective investigative and sanctioning powers.


Refer this petition to the appropriate Standing Committee for detailed review, taking into account the accompanying policy framework and draft principles submitted in support of this petition.

## 9. Closing
Public trust in digital governance, economic development, and national digital infrastructure depends fundamentally on the existence of strong legal safeguards for personal data.
We respectfully urge the People’s Majlis to recognise the national importance of this issue and to take timely legislative action to protect the privacy, dignity, and security of the people of the Maldives.
We look forward to the Honourable Majlis’ favourable consideration of this petition.
